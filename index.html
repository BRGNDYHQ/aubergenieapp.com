What should I cook tonight?

Two ways to answer it: ask for something new, or pick from what you've already saved.

Say what you fancy.

Type a dinner and Aubergenie AI writes the whole recipe, around the allergies, diet and servings you've saved. It can get this wrong, so if you have an allergy, check every ingredient and label yourself.

Tell it once how you eat, and it remembers: allergies, diet, dislikes, what's in your kitchen, how many you cook for.

ExampleAubergenie will leave out peanuts, keep things vegetarian, make use of your air fryer and size recipes for four.

Every recipe it writes keeps to that. So does Plan My Week, which proposes nothing that mentions an allergy you've named, and uses no AI at all.

Aubergenie's cloud AI runs only when you ask, with Sign in with Apple; the recipes are written by Anthropic's Claude on Aubergenie's server. Switch it off in Settings and everything else still works. Recipes it writes use an allowance you can see in the app.

Or pick from your own.

Your own library, with the app's filters. One tap narrows it to what you can face tonight. These nine are from ours.

From plan to plate.

Put meals on days, shop one list, then cook in big type. The week carries you to dinner.

  1. Plan the week.

    Put meals on days, or let Plan My Week suggest them from the recipes you've saved. Leftovers land on the days they'll be eaten, and Today shows what's next.

  2. Shop one list.

    Everything for the week, merged and in aisle order. Tick things off in the shop; with iCloud on, the list follows you between your own Apple devices.

  3. Cook in big type.

    One step at a time, and a timer wherever the recipe says so. With alarms allowed it rings even when your iPhone is locked, and shows in the Dynamic Island on iPhones that have one. On a Mac, timers arrive as notifications.

    At the end: You made it, and a photograph while it looks its best, which becomes the recipe's photo. The recipe keeps the rest too: how many times you've made it, your rating, your notes for next time. The plates on this page are the cook's own, from the recipes in the app.

Send recipes, not screenshots.

Photo, amounts, method and timers, in one file. A friend with Aubergenie saves it in a tap. Anyone else gets a picture or the text.

Your recipes stay on your devices and in your own iCloud. No ads, no tracking. Your name travels with a recipe only when you switch that on.
